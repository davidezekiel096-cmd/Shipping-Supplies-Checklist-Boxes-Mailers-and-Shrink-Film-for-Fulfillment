## Film and Bundling

[Shrink film](https://www.theboxery.com/catalog.asp?d=106035&name=Shrink+Film) is a plastic film that tightens around a product when heat is applied. It is used to bundle items, protect them from dust and moisture, and keep multi-piece sets together. Stretch wrap works differently: it stretches and clings by hand or machine and needs no heat, which makes it the usual choice for bundling and securing pallets.

- [ ] Decide which sets need bundling
- [ ] Order film in the right width for the product

## Packing Steps

1. Place the product in the box or mailer.
2. Add padding on every side.
3. Close the box and seal the center seam and both edges.
4. Shake gently to check for movement.
5. Apply the label on a flat surface.

## Troubleshooting

- **Product moves inside the box:** add void fill or switch to a smaller box.
- **Corners crush in transit:** raise the ECT rating or add corner padding.
- **Mailer seam splits:** choose a larger size or a padded mailer for heavier items.
- **Seal opens:** reseal the center seam and both edges with shipping tape.
- **Item arrives damp:** switch to a poly mailer or add a moisture barrier.

## Glossary

- **ECT:** edge crush test, a measure of corrugated board strength
- **Corrugated:** board made from a fluted layer between flat liners
- **Void fill:** material that fills empty space inside a box
- **Kraft:** strong paper, usually brown, used for mailers and wrapping
- **Poly:** polyethylene plastic
- **Landed cost:** unit price plus shipping, plus the value of time saved
- **Inner dimensions:** the measurements of the space inside a box

## Storage

Store boxes flat and dry, away from direct heat and damp. Keep mailer cases closed until needed, label them by size, and use older stock first. A clean, dry storage area keeps board stiff and keeps adhesive strips ready to seal.

## Planning Ahead

Demand for packaging rises before peak shopping periods, so ordering ahead keeps packing lines moving. Check stock levels a few weeks before a promotion or holiday, order the sizes that sell most, and keep one spare case of the most-used mailer.

## Common Questions

**How much padding is enough?** Enough that the product cannot touch the walls of the box. Pad every side, including the top and bottom.

**Does a bigger box mean better protection?** No. A box that is too large lets the product move, so choose the smallest box that holds the product and its padding.

**What does a bubble lining do?** It cushions the product against bumps and drops, and it adds a layer of protection against punctures.

**When is a poly mailer enough?** For clothing and soft goods that need no extra padding.

**How often should supplies be reordered?** Set a threshold for each size, such as two weeks of average orders, and reorder when stock reaches it.
